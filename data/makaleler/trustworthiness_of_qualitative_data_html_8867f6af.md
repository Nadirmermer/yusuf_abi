### Giriş

Psychology»Research Methodology

Trustworthiness inqualitative researchis akin to the concepts ofvalidity and reliabilityin quantitative research.

It refers to the degree of confidence researchers have in the accuracy and truthfulness of their findings.

Egon Guba proposed a model for evaluating the trustworthiness of naturalistic inquiries, which is often applied to qualitative research. The model consists of four key criteria:

- Credibility(parallel to internal validity): Ensuring the findings accurately represent the reality of the participants, achieved through methods like prolonged engagement, peer debriefing, and member checking.

- Transferability(parallel to external validity): Providing sufficient contextual information to allow readers to judge whether findings can be applied to other settings.

- Dependability(parallel to reliability): Demonstrating the research process is logical, traceable, and clearly documented.

- Confirmability(parallel to objectivity): Ensuring findings emerge from the data rather than researcher bias, through methods like audit trails and reflexivity.

While these criteria are presented as distinct, they are interconnected and mutually supportive.

They highlight the importance ofrigor, transparency, andreflexivityin conducting and reporting qualitative studies.

By striving to meet these criteria, researchers can enhance the credibility and confidence in the findings of their qualitative research, contributing to a more trustworthy and meaningful body of knowledge.

Policymakers and practitioners rely on trustworthy research to make informed decisions.

Trustworthy qualitative studies provide valuable insights into human experiences and perspectives, which can be used to develop effective policies and improve service delivery.

### 1. Credibility (Truth Value)

Credibility, akin tointernal validity in quantitative research, focuses on establishing confidence in the ‘truth’ of the findings.

It focuses on the accuracy and plausibility of the findings and demonstrates that findings represent the true picture of the phenomenon.

It assesses whether the findings reflect the participants’ perspectives and experiences in a meaningful and believable way within the context in which the study took place.

### Strategies for enhancing credibility include:

- Prolonged engagement:Spending sufficient time in the field to build rapport with participants and gain a deep understanding of their perspectives.

- Persistent observation:Carefully and consistently observing the phenomena of interest to ensure thorough data collection.

- Triangulation:Using multiple data sources, methods, or researchers to corroborate findings and enhance their credibility.

- Member checking:Allowing participants to review and verify the accuracy of the data, interpretations, and conclusions.

### 2. Transferability (Applicability)

Transferabilityis similar toexternal validityin quantitative research, and examines the extent to which the findings of a particular inquiry can be applied to other contexts or settings.

While generalizability is often not the goal in qualitative research, transferability seeks to determine if findings “fit into contexts outside the study situation that are determined by the degree of similarity or goodness of fit between the two contexts”.

Transferability is primarily the responsibility of the reader who wants to apply the findings to a new situation.

### Strategies for enhancing transferability include:

- Providing rich,thick descriptionsof the research context and participants.

- Clearly outlining thesampling proceduresto show how participants were selected.

- Discussing the characteristics of the sample and their relevance to other groups.

### 3. Dependability (Consistency)

Dependabilityis comparable toreliabilityin quantitative research, and addresses the stability and consistency of the research findings over time.

It seeks to ensure that if the study were replicated with similar participants in a comparable context, consistent findings would emerge.

### Strategies for enhancing transferability include:

- Maintaining a comprehensiveaudit trail of all research decisions and modifications.

- Employing systematic documentation methods, including dense descriptions of research procedures and the use of consistent coding techniques.

- Engaging in peer debriefing and utilizing strategies like stepwise replication or code-recode procedures to assess the stability of the findings.

### 4. Confirmability (Neutrality)

This criterion, replacing the concept of objectivity in quantitative research, emphasizes the degree to which the findings are a product of the data and not influenced by the researcher’s biases or interpretations.

Confirmabilityaims to demonstrate that the findings emerge from the data and not their own predispositions.

### Strategies for enhancing confirmability include:

- Peer debriefing:Consulting with colleagues or experts to verify interpretations and minimize researcher bias.

- Member checking:Seeking participant feedback to ensure the findings accurately reflect their experiences.

- Reflexive journaling:Documenting researcher reflections and biases to enhance transparency and mitigate subjectivity.
