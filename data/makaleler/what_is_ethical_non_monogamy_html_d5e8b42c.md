Ethical non-monogamy is a relationship structure where all involved parties consent to have multiple romantic or sexual relationships, with honesty and open communication. Unlikecheating, all actions are aboveboard and ethically conducted, withrespect for everyone’s boundariesand feelings.

### Key Points

- Umbrella Term:Ethical non-monogamy (ENM) covers relationships that are not exclusive between two people, as long as everyone involved consents.

- Vs. Monogamy:In monogamy, two partners are exclusive with one another and do not pursue romantic or intimate relationships with anyone else.

- Consent and Honesty:People who practice ENM are expected to be considerate and honest with every partner involved.

- Who’s Involved:One or both partners can pursue outside connections, and a person may also be involved with someone who has multiple partners of their own.

- Not Cheating:ENM differs from cheating because everyone consents to, and is happy with, the arrangement.

### Ethical non-monogamy Vs. non-monogamy

ENM differs from non-monogamy in general in one key way:agreed consentbetween everyone involved in the relationship.

In contrast, without the word ethical, people can engage in outside relations without the knowledge or consent of everyone involved.

ENM is also not the same as infidelity or cheating.

Infidelity means that a partner in an exclusive relationship has a romantic or intimate relationship with someone outside their relationship without the knowledge or consent of their partner.

When someone is cheating, they are not communicating ethically. They arelying,deceiving, and disrespecting their partner.

### Is ethical non-monogamy Accepted?

Society often has it ingrained in us that there is a set structure for how relationships ought to work, as well as establishing what is acceptable and unacceptable.

Society and the media will usually display monogamous relationships as the norm and something everyone should strive for, and many people may not realize that there are other options.

Since the concept is thought to be relatively new, it can be difficult to breach the topic of ENM with friends and family.

Many people who engage in ENM may do so discreetly because they fear the judgment that may follow if they share that their relationships are ENM.

There is a harmful stereotype that those who engage in ENM are cheating or do not truly care for their partners. That fear is real. That stereotype can make it difficult for people to share details about their relationships.

### Types of Ethical Non-Monogamy

ENM is an umbrella term for many types of relationships. ENM can be practiced without following a specific relationship model and can comprise whatever rules that all parties agree on.

Nonetheless, there are different types of ENM relationships, which are described below:

### Polyamory

Polyamorymeans having more than one relationship – these can be romantic or intimate in nature.

Triad relationships are those composed of 3 partners. A quad would consist of 4 partners.

In other cases, one person may have their ownsecondary partnersseparate from theirprimary partner(the prioritized partner).

The primary and secondary partners may also or may not have their own additional partners.

### Open relationships

Opening the relationshipmeans that one or both partners can pursue sex and sometimes emotional attachments to other people outside the relationship.

The difference to polyamory is that relations outside of the primary relationship are typically casual andnon-committal. The primary partner remains the most prioritized.

### Monogamish

This means a couple is monogamous most of the time but will occasionally take part in outside relations.

This is usually not often, for example, a couple may engage in sexual or other romantic relations with outsiders once and then not again for 5 years or more.

### Relationship anarchy

Most people place their romantic relationship above all other relationships, including friendships and family. Relationship anarchists reject that ranking. They treat all types of relationships as equally important.

They do not automatically prioritize their spouse over their platonic friendships for example. This means they would not use the terms ‘primary/secondary partner/s.’

They may only choose to have one romantic partner but they are considered ENM as they value outside relationships as equally important as their romantic ones.

### Polygamy

Polygamy is marriage to more than one spouse at a time.Polygynyis one husband with multiple wives;polyandryis one wife with multiple husbands.

Polygamy appears in a number of religious texts, and some sects still practice it today.

It can be practiced ethically. Some documented cases, though, have involved coercion and exploitation (Heath et al., 2016).

It is only ethical when every party genuinely consents to the arrangement.

### Polyfidelity

This type of ENM is where there can be multiple romantic or sexual partners who are restricted to romantic or sexual activity only to members within the group.

The group may be open to allow other members to join, but there are often no romantic or sexual encounters with the new addition until they are added.

### ENM Marriage

ENM marriage refers to a marital arrangement where both spouses agree, with transparency and consent, to engage in multiple romantic or sexual relationships outside of their union.

This approach emphasizes open communication, honesty, and respect for all involved parties, distinguishing it from infidelity.

The specifics of the arrangement can vary, including polyamory, open marriages, andswinging, among others. Each version looks different. Swinging is couples having sex with other people, often as a shared activity, without polyamory’s deeper emotional attachment.

### Why choose ethical non-monogamy?

There are many reasons why someone may choose to engage in ENM, including the following:

### To fulfill other needs

People who engage in ENM may do so to meet other needs they may have that their present relationship cannot provide.

Often, in monogamous relationships, there is the expectation that your one partner must fulfill every need that you have for the relationship to work.

Not every need fits one person.

Many who are ENM believe that putting these expectations on a partner is unreasonable. You cannot expect one person to meet every need you have, just as they cannot expect everything from you.

It can be a burden on others and can lead to disappointment or relationship breakdowns. That is a lot to ask of one person.

ENM allows people to look for others with similar romantic or sexual interests that their partner does not have or is not comfortable exploring.

This also allows your partner the chance to explore things that you may not be interested in. This is not to say that their relationship lacks something, but that there is more to gain from others to strengthen it.

“I am so happy this person makes them happy too, because it doesn’t take away from what I give them – that is something we had – which is sometimes hard to remember”

### To strengthen their relationship

While it might sound unusual, engaging in relationships outside of the primary partner might strengthen the relationship they have together.

Those who engage in ENM may have strong communication skills and high levels of honesty with their partner. They may not feel the need to hide anything.

“I love the growth part of it. I don’t think there’s anything where you grow as much, where you have to reflect as much, be aware of yourself as much and be forced to communicate as much”.

### To explore their sexuality

Some people may not have had the chance to explore their sexuality before settling down with one person.

They may be extremely happy with their current partner. Even so, they may feel they missed out on experimenting with people of different genders if they never had the chance.

ENM may then make sense for someone to explore their sexuality without having to give up the relationship they have.

### To share the love they can offer

Some people who engage in ENM point to human nature. They argue it is normal to want more than one romantic connection.

It is already normal to love more than one friend, family member, or child at once. Why, they ask, should romantic relationships be so restricted?

They may feel that having one romantic relationship limits the amount of love they have to share with others. Some people feel most like themselves when they can love two or more people.

### To not have to end a relationship

Some people have a very strong relationship with their partner but find they are attracted to someone else too.

They do not want to end it, or to cheat. But monogamous norms pressure them to pick one person.

Many people end strong relationships for exactly this reason, believing they must choose one partner or the other.

ENM offers another option: pursuing both, as long as everyone involved discusses and consents to the arrangement.

### Challenges of Ethical Non-Monogamy

Below are some considerations and potential limitations of engaging in ENM:

### Social stigma and judgment

People who engage in ENM may face judgment from those outside of the community, including family members, friends, and therapists.

The stigma associated with ENM can make it difficult to be open about relationship choices and could lead to feelings of shame and isolation.

For example,one participant in a studyshared that a negative experience involved “dealing with family members when they found out about our lifestyle and we were judged by them. It was none of their business.”

### Jealousy and insecurity

Even in consensual ENM,jealousyandinsecuritycan arise, even when individuals consciously choose to live this way.

One or more partner may have been enthusiastic about the idea of ENM, but then later discovered that they do not like ‘sharing’ their partner with others.

Many people who practice ENM describe the flip side of that discomfort ascompersion. It’s surprisingly common. Compersion is a feeling of genuine pleasure in a partner’s happiness with someone else, rather than threat or loss.

It is not automatic, and where it develops, it usually comes gradually, through repeated experience and reflection on what a person actually wants for a partner.

### Communication challenges

ENM relationships require open, honest communication and ongoing negotiation to thrive.

People who engage in this need to make sure their partner/s are trustworthy.

Navigating multiple relationships can be complex, and without clear communication and agreed-upon boundaries, misunderstandings and hurt feelings are more likely.

“I think people have to be honest, and not all people are honest. So that can ruin it.”

### Potential for exploitation and abuse

As with any relationship style, there is a potential fortoxicorabusive relationshipsin ENM.

A relationship with one partner can start off looking healthy. Later on, that person may show their true colors, become controlling, or try to isolate someone from their other partner or partners.

It’s essential for individuals to be aware of power dynamics, consent, and their own boundaries within these relationships, and to keep reflecting on whether this lifestyle is suited to them.

### Practicing Ethical Non-Monogamy

If you are already in an exclusive relationship but want to try ENM, there are some steps you can take. Here is how to raise it with your partner:

### Ensure the relationship you have is strong

ENM is not for everyone, and it may not be suitable for some couples, especially if they are experiencing relationship difficulties.

Remember that ENM will not fix a relationship that is already breaking down. Make sure your relationship is strong, and resolve any existing issues, before introducing ENM.

### Consider your capacity

It’s essential to consider whether you have the capacity in your life for additional connections or relationships.

While love is not a limited resource, you may have limited time oremotional bandwidth. That means the amount of room you feel you have in your life foremotional attachments.

“Be very careful to not overwhelm and overbook yourself to the point you’re neglecting others and your own well-being. While love may be a limitless resource, time and attention are not.”

### Discuss what you would both like to gain

Communicate openly and honestly with your partner about what you would like and offer them the opportunity to also express what they want.

Discuss what kind of relationship you would like to get into and the aspects of it that would suit both of you. You can discuss timeframes and long-term relationship goals to aim for.

### Reassure your partner

Your partner may feel taken aback by your proposal of engaging in ENM. It is important to reassure them that you do not want to seek other relationships because they lack something.

Reassure them that with ENM, you can both add more to an already strong and healthy relationship.

### Set clear boundaries

Ensure that you and your partner set clear boundaries and expectations for what is allowed in the relationship.

The boundaries can help protect you from the fears you may have. You and your partner can make a list of what you are fearful of and make it a rule that those boundaries are not crossed into outside relationships.

“A boundary is simply this: it is one thing, it is something that you put on yourself. […] Your boundary cannot be ‘my boundary is for my partner to do this’ or ‘my partner not to do that.'”

### Negotiate

You cannot always meet everyone’s needs perfectly. It helps to negotiate a place in the middle: one partner may be ‘all in’ while the other stays more cautious.

Communication works best when everyone feels heard, with as little judgment as possible.

Consider whether both of you can consent to the process, or whether one partner feels ‘dragged along.’ Listen to what your partner needs, and negotiate with care.

### Connect with others

If you and your partner are debating ENM, consider connecting with others who practice ENM and can help in your research of how it works for them.

You can get an understanding of their opinions and their advice on how to go about it before deciding if it is right for you.

### Manage new relationship energy (NRE)

New Relationship Energy (NRE) is a concept often discussed in the context of ENM and polyamory, though it applies to all types of relationships.

It is that giddy, excited feeling you get when things are going well with a new relationship.

However, NRE is temporary and you should be mindful of this when practicing ENM.

“Enjoying that satisfying feeling [of NRE] is perfectly fine, but it can come with some unintended side-effects. Make sure you’re not chasing NRE only to abandon people when the feeling inevitably wears off or at least diminishes.”

### Check-in with each other

Whilst engaging in ENM, ensure you check in with your partner regularly.

Make sure they are still consenting to the relationship style and whether there is anything to work on or other boundaries to be put in place.

### Can ethically non-monogamous people get jealous?

While ENM people can get jealous, many who practice ENM believe monogamous relationships can often be filled with more jealousy or possessive feelings.

ENM often involves letting go of extreme jealous feelings and instead learning to be happy that your partner is doing something which makes them happy.

Many believe jealousy is auseful emotionas it gives people a chance to learn about what they need to feel secure.

Some people who are ENM may get jealous while others do not – what matters is how jealousy is handled and ensuring boundaries are in place to manage this.

### Are there any limits to a non-monogamous relationship?

Every relationship is unique in its own way. Boundaries can differ from one relationship to the next, so what might be ok in one relationship may not be ok in the next.

It is up to each partner to communicate their desires and limits, with those limits being respected.

### Can ethical non-monogamy be sustainable?

Many people practice ENM for years or longer if this is what all parties involved want.

Research suggests that in ENM and monogamous relationships, there are similar levels of satisfaction, happiness, and relationship longevity in both.

Making time for self-reflection, having realistic expectations, and checking in with your partners can help to ensure the relationships last.

Exploring ENM does not mean you can never return to a monogamous lifestyle. It is possible to be ENM for a short amount of time, then return to being exclusive to one person.

It could be that one person will want to return to monogamy. In contrast, the other wants to stay ENM. These differences can and do sometimes mean that the relationship needs to come to an end, as they can with other types of disagreements not limited to relationship structure.

### How can you bring up ethical non-monogamy to a partner?

Consider whether the interest in ENM is equally shared and whether you have the communication skills to broach the subject in a gentle way that still reassures your partner.

Considerate,non-judgmental sharing and listeningare usually key.

Whatever the nature of the relationships, it is essential to talk about boundaries and expectations, including those related to shared space, allocated time, and acceptable behavior.

### What is the difference between polyamory and ethical non-monogamy?

ENM is an umbrella term for many types of relationship structures, and polyamory is just one way to practice it.

Polyamoryrefers to having multiple romantic or intimate relationships at the same time, whereas ENM is any arrangement where people have multiple consensual romantic or sexual connections.

### Research on Ethical Non-Monogamy

A growing body of research looks at how people practicing ENM actually fare, and at what happens when ENM meets institutions built around a single-partner assumption. The most recent work spans direct outcome studies and applied research on healthcare and therapy.

### Relationship Quality Compared With Monogamy

Gupta and colleagues reviewed 209 published studies on polyamory and consensual non-monogamy for a wide-ranging scoping review. They coded each study for research design, theoretical framework, and findings, sorting the literature into seven broad domains (Gupta et al., 2023).

Within the outcomes domain, one picture emerged. People practicing consensual non-monogamy reported relationship satisfaction, psychological wellbeing, and relational functioning broadly comparable to monogamous comparison groups.

That does not mean every relationship structure works identically. What differs most consistently across monogamy, open relationships, polyamory, and swinging is not psychological wellbeing, but the specific practice a couple has actually agreed to.

Disclosure is a separate question entirely.

The review carries one clear limitation.

Most outcome studies rely on people who already identify comfortably as non-monogamous to a researcher. That likely excludes people practicing it privately, or without a settled label (Gupta et al., 2023).

### Stigma in Healthcare and Therapy

Stigma toward ENM extends beyond friends and family.

Campbell and colleagues surveyed and interviewed 67 consensually non-monogamous adults about their trust in healthcare professionals and their disclosure decisions (Campbell et al., 2023).

Participants reported significantly lower trust in healthcare professionals than standard comparison scores. Sixty-three percent disclosed their relationship structure when accessing sexual healthcare. Thirty-seven percent sometimes or never did, often to avoid anticipated judgment.

Therapy carries the same pattern. A UK study of 19 consensually non-monogamous adults found that mono-normativity operates on both sides of the room. Clients anticipate judgment. Some practitioners treat the relationship structure itself as something to correct, rather than a legitimate choice (Swindlehurst et al., 2023).

Both research teams recommend dedicated training for clinicians and therapists working with consensually non-monogamous clients, rather than leaving patients to manage the stigma alone. Neither study followed clients over time to test whether such training actually changes outcomes, a natural next step for future research.

### What Relationship Experts Say

Esther Perel is a couples therapist and host of the podcastWhere Should We Begin?She says polyamory now comes up in nearly every professional conversation she has about sexuality and monogamy.

### Polyamory as Distributed Roles, Not Commitment Avoidance

As Perel explains, polyamory is a form of consensual non-monogamy marked by emotional, and often romantic, attachment on top of any sexual involvement.

That sets it apart from purely sexual arrangements.

Perel directly challenges the idea that polyamory reflects an inability to commit. Sustaining several conscious, negotiated relationships, she argues, takes more communication and effort than a single relationship, not less.

She describes polyamory as a kind of emotional “outsourcing.” Rather than expecting one partner to be a best friend, co-parent, and ideal sexual partner all at once, polyamory distributes those roles across more than one relationship.

The communal side is often overlooked too.

According to Perel, polyamorous people tend to be closely networked, with their own conferences, support groups, and shared parenting arrangements.

### When “Ethical” Breaks Down

Perel also works through cases where the “ethical” half of ethical non-monogamy fails in practice. Take one caller’s case. She believed, for two years, that she was in a consensual polyamorous relationship.

It was not. Her partner’s other partner had never been told about, or agreed to, the relationship at all.

Perel told the caller she had “kept her compass” throughout, even while being deliberately misled. As Perel frames it, what separates ethical non-monogamy from infidelity is not how many people are involved. It is whether everyone affected genuinely knows, and has consented.

A relationship described using the language of polyamory can still function as concealed cheating, in Perel’s reading, if that consent was never real. That is the whole distinction the word “ethical” is doing.
